# std.channels: Channels
status: implemented
**Extension points.** Module manifest: stdlib module `channels` (capabilities `clock`, `net`, `observe`), served from a `node` module's contract (std.contracts). Check rule registry: E2601 and E2602 through `@keel/std-channels/tools`.

**Declare.** In a `*.schema.ts` file whose name is the export:
```ts
export const boardChannel = channel('todos.board', { path: '/api/board/events', params: BoardParams, message: BoardMessage })
```
`channel(name, { path, params, message? })`: `name` is prefixed by the declaring module, as E1155 asks of route ids. `params` is parsed from the stream URL's search (fail: 422 `keel.fields`) and picks an instance. `message` (optional) is the schema of what members publish; without it nothing can be published (E2602: no `s.secret`). Its `events` and `body` are its entries' schemas (std.contracts).

**Serve.** In a `*.server.ts` file (else E2601):
```ts
export const boardEvents = channelRoute(api, boardChannel, { id: 'api.boardEvents', publish: 'api.boardPublish', stores: boardStores, follows: [boardDb], member: ({ caller, user, params, stores }) => true })
```
`stores` and `follows` are optional, a route having either, both or neither. `follows` lists data capability accessors (k.data) whose object has `follow(channel, key, on)`; an entry without one is a type error. `channelRoute` returns `{ loader, action }`, bound at `api`'s entries `id` and `publish` (std.contracts): a live loader (k.server) yielding `ChannelEvent<M>` and the publish action:
- `{ kind: 'reset' }`, first on every open: the client re-reads; nothing missed while closed is resent.
- `{ kind: 'saved', store, head }` after each save of a store `stores` creates (k.sync `save`), in save order, to every open member stream.
- `{ kind: 'changed' }` after each committed change a capability in `follows` reports for this channel and the stream's params (std.postgres-changes `changed`), to every open member stream of them, with `member` re-run first as after a save.
- `{ kind: 'message', message }` for each publish to the same channel and params, the publisher's stream included.
- `{ kind: 'closed', reason }`, then the end: `denied` when `member` is or turns false, `full` when the open is past a cap.

`member` is a pure predicate over the request's `caller` and `user` (k.user), the parsed `params` and `stores` opened read-only. It runs at open (false: `closed` `denied` is the only event), at each publish (false: 403, nothing delivered) and, for each open stream, after each save of its stores and each followed change and before each delivery. Before each delivery to an open stream (`saved`, `changed` or `message`) the route calls `currentUser()` (k.user); an answer other than the stream's `user` gives `{ kind: 'closed', reason: 'denied' }` in place of that event, then the end. An answer given less than 1,000 ms earlier on `ctx.clock` for that stream stands, so a burst of saves costs one `identify` per stream per second. With `currentUser.fixed` (no identity source) the route asks nothing: no check, clock read or timer on a delivery. Deliveries to one stream keep their order; the checks of different streams run independently, so one slow `identify` delays only its own stream. A publisher's own `currentUser()` is not asked: a publish is a request and runs `identify` as one. `changed` and `saved` are delivered in the order they arrive at the process. At open, the stream follows each `follows` entry, and a route with `stores` whose `ctx.storage` has `watch` (k.persist Watch) is watching it, before it yields `reset`; an entry or watch not listening within 5,000 ms on `ctx.clock`, or a `lost` report from either, ends the stream with no `closed` event, as a drop, so `subscribe` reopens with its backoff. A process holds one watch for its streams of routes with `stores`, started with the first and stopped with the last. An event-stream GET reads the caller cookie and never sets one (k.server). The POST takes `{ message }` as an action: the k.server same-site proof, `maxBodyBytes` and 422 `keel.fields` apply, and it answers `ok({})` once delivered. A followed change reaches the member streams open in every process whose capability shares the database. A save reaches the member streams open in the saving process, by its `save` event, and in each other process watching the same data, by a `committed` report naming the store's key: the route reads that key, in the read `member` shares, and delivers `saved` with the stored snapshot's `head`. A process delivers a `saved` of a store only when its head is above the last `saved` of that store it delivered, so on a stream one store's heads strictly rise and one head may stand for several saves. A head below the last `saved` of that store the stream delivered (the store's count restarted because its storage was replaced, k.sync Heads) is delivered as `{ kind: 'reset' }` in its place, and the stream counts from that head on. After a stream's `reset`, each save of a store in its `stores`, by any process on the same data, is followed on that stream by a `saved` of that store with that head or a later one, or by the stream's end. On a storage without `watch` a save reaches the saving process's streams only. A `message` reaches the member streams open in the process that serves its publish and no other; nothing stores, notifies or resends it, so a stream closed, reopening or on another replica then never gets it. A publish served after a save of a store in `stores` resolved in the same process reaches each stream after that save's `saved` or `reset`, if any. What members on every replica must see is a save of a store in `stores` or a followed `changed`. Caps on open streams and what a full open gets: std.channels-limits.

**Client.**
- `subscribe(ctx, origin, channel, params, on)`, inside a scope: reads the live loader through a k.services `events: true` endpoint of the channel at `origin` and calls `on(event)` for each `ok` event. After the iteration ends (a drop, `api.network`, `closed` `full`) it reopens 1 s later, doubling to 30 s on `ctx.clock`; it makes no request while `ctx.net.online()` is false and reconnects on `{ kind: 'net', online: true }`. Returns `{ status, close }`: `status()` reads a signal, `'connecting' | 'open' | 'offline' | 'denied'`; after `closed` `denied` it stops. Disposing the scope closes the stream.
- `publish(ctx, origin, channel, params, message)`: `Promise<Result<null, ApiError>>`, through `@keel/services` (k.services errors and timeout).

**Privacy.** Events carry no caller, cookie or store state: `saved` names the store and its head, `changed` names nothing (neither the capability nor the params nor a row), and each member re-reads through its own loader. A message reaches every member's browser as published, with no publisher identity unless its schema holds one; it is never stored, logged or resent. `stores` and `member` stay on the server.

**Rules.** E2601 `channelRoute` outside a `*.server.ts` file · E2602 a channel `message` schema holding an `s.secret`.

**Conformance.**
- A member's stream starts with `{ kind: 'reset' }` and then gets `{ kind: 'saved', store, head }` after each save of a store its channel's `stores` creates, in save order.
- A caller for whom `member` is false at open gets `{ kind: 'closed', reason: 'denied' }` as its only event; a cookieless open has caller `""` and its response sets no cookie.
- After a save that makes `member` false for an open stream's caller, that stream gets `{ kind: 'closed', reason: 'denied' }` and ends, and the other members' streams continue.
- A member's publish delivers `{ kind: 'message', message }` to every open member stream of the same channel and params, the publisher's included, and to none with other params; a non-member's publish is 403 and delivers nothing.
- A publish without same-site proof is 403 `keel.denied` and one failing `message` is 422 `keel.fields`; neither delivers anything.
- No delivered event holds a caller hash, a cookie value or a store's state.
- Under sim, `subscribe` reopens 1 s, 2 s, 4 s after successive drops on `ctx.clock`, sends nothing while `online()` is false, reconnects on `{ kind: 'net', online: true }`, and its `status()` passes through `connecting`, `open`, `offline` and `denied` as those happen.
- Disposing the scope that called `subscribe` aborts its request, and `on` gets no later event.
- An open past `perCaller` streams for one caller gets `{ kind: 'closed', reason: 'full' }` as its only event, and the earlier streams continue.
- `channel` throws E2602 for a message schema with a nested `s.secret`; `keel check` reports E2601 and E2602 on fixtures and nothing on their clean twins.
- An open past `perAnonymous` cookieless streams, from any client, gets `{ kind: 'closed', reason: 'full' }` as its only event, and the earlier streams continue; with that bound reached, a caller with a cookie opens up to its `perCaller`.
- With two serving modules, each counts only the streams on its own channel routes against its `maxStreams` and `perCaller`: one at its `maxStreams` leaves the other's opens unaffected.
- With `follows: [db]`, an open member stream gets `reset`, then `{ kind: 'changed' }` after a committed `changed` of its channel and params, and nothing for other params; a stream for which `member` is false at that point gets `closed` `denied`.
- A `lost` report ends each following stream with no `closed` event; under sim, `subscribe` reopens 1 s later and the new stream starts with `reset`.
- No `changed` event holds a capability name, a param or a row value.
- With `identify` answering `'u1'` for a stream's cookie, then `''` once that session is removed, the next save of its stores gives that stream `{ kind: 'closed', reason: 'denied' }` and no `saved`; another member's stream gets `saved`.
- Five saves within 500 ms on the sim clock run `identify` once for an open stream; a save 1,000 ms after the check runs it again.
- `identify` throwing or not settling within 5,000 ms at a delivery closes that stream `denied`.
- Under sim, with `createApp` A on ctx `a` and B on `b = replica(a)` (k.sim Replicas) serving one channel route with `stores`, a member stream open on B gets `reset`, then `{ kind: 'saved', store, head }` after an action on A saves that store, `head` being the head A's save produced.
- Under sim, with A and B as above, for a generated sequence of saves of one store by actions on A and on B, the `saved` heads on each member stream open on A or on B strictly rise, and once the run settles the last equals the store's head.
- Under sim, with A and B as above, a member stream open on B for which `member` turns false after a save on A gets `{ kind: 'closed', reason: 'denied' }` in place of that save's `saved`, then its end; the events before it are those of earlier saves.
- Under sim, with A and B as above, a member's publish on A delivers `{ kind: 'message', message }` to A's member streams of that channel and params and to none of B's.
- Under sim, with one `createApp` serving a channel route whose `stores` creates store `s` and a member stream open, an action that saves `s` to head `h` and answers, then a member's publish of `m` to that channel and params, give that stream `{ kind: 'saved', store: 's', head: h }` and then `{ kind: 'message', message: m }`, in that order.
- Under sim, a member stream opened after a publish of `m` to its channel and params was answered `ok` gets `{ kind: 'reset' }` first and never a `{ kind: 'message', message: m }` for that publish.
- On a real ctx whose storage driver has a `watch` the test controls, two member streams of routes with `stores` call `watch` once; neither gets `reset` before it resolves; `on({ kind: 'lost' })` ends both with no `closed` event; a stream opened after that calls `watch` again; and the function `watch` resolved runs when the last stream ends.
- On a real ctx whose storage driver has no `watch`, a member stream of a route with `stores` gets `reset` at open.
