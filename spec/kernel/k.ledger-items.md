# k.ledger-items: Ledger items and runs
status: implemented
How `keel ledger` (k.ledger) names a spec clause, how a conformance test cites one, which projects it reads, and which run makes a test passing.

**Clause key.** A clause is one bullet of a section's Conformance list; its text is the bullet after `- `. Its normal form is that text with Markdown backticks dropped, `\"` and typographic quotes made straight, each run of white space made one space, the ends trimmed, lower case, and one final period dropped. Its item is `<section>#<key>`, `<key>` the first 8 lowercase hex digits of the SHA-256 of the normal form's UTF-8 bytes (`k.router#3f9a1b2c`). Its position `n`, 1-based among the section's bullets, is for people: a clause's `ledger` record also holds `n`, and a diagnostic about a clause names it `<section>#<n>` at its bullet's line. Moving a bullet, or adding or removing another, changes `n` and no item. A rewording beyond backticks, quote style, spacing, case and the final period is a new item: `--diff` reads the old item as removed and the new one as added, and no evidence carries over. Two bullets of one section with one normal form are E1733 at the second; the ledger holds one item for them.

**Citation.** A conformance test file (`packages/<pkg>/conformance/**`) cites a clause with a header line `// Spec: <section>, clause "<quote>"`, one clause per line; the header is the file's `//` lines before its first statement other than an import. The quote opens at the `"` after `clause ` and closes at the next `"` with no `\` before it, so a `"` of the clause is written `\"`. A quote still open at the end of its line goes on in the line after it when that is a `//` line, the two joined by one space. Text after the closing quote on its line is a note (`(E2003)`, `(rfc:0079)`) and holds no `"`. The quote cites the clause of that section whose normal form is the quote's normal form. A `// Spec:` line of any other form, one naming no section, or one whose quote cites no bullet of its section is E1732 at that line. No other comment is read, a quote of a section's other paragraphs among them.

**Projects.** A project is a folder whose tests and reports the ledger reads: the folder `keel ledger` runs in, and each `packages/<p>`, `apps/<a>` and `examples/<e>` in it holding a `package.json`. A project's name is its path from where `keel ledger` runs, `.` for that folder itself. Its evidence is its own: its run reports in `reports/tests/` (k.test-run Run report), its `reports/a11y.json` (k.a11y), its `reports/mutation/mutation.json` and its `mutation-justified.json` (k.ledger Mutation). The ledger file names a project by that name in `a11y` items (`a11y:<project>:<routeId>#<criterion>`), in `mutation.projects` and in W1733's path; a mutation claim's `file` is its path from where `keel ledger` runs, an app project's mutated files being its `modules/**`; `exports` alone label a package or app by its folder name (`pkg`, k.ledger File). An installed library is not a project of the app: `libraries` lists it by package name with the counts of the ledger it ships (k.library Ledger). A library's own folder, where its `keel ledger` runs, is the project `.`. An app outside this repository is the project `.`: its rungs rest on its own `reports/`, its a11y items are `a11y:.:…`, its mutation is `mutation.projects['.']`, and its libraries are listed beside them.

**Run evidence.** A test is passing when its project's run reports hold its file with the `sha` of the file's current source and hold, at a `line` from the first to the last line of the test's call, at least one test `passed` and none `failed`. Any other test gives no evidence, whatever its text; "a passing test" in k.ledger means this. A project with a test file the ledger reads and no file in `reports/tests/`, or whose reports lack such a file, hold it with another `sha`, or hold it in two report files, is one W1733 at `<project>/reports/tests/`, naming how many test files and the first one; their tests give no evidence. A project with a page route and no `reports/a11y.json` is W1733 at that path, and its a11y items are `none`. Reports are a run's output and are not committed: the committed ledger is written from the reports of one run of every project, on the tree it is committed with.

**Rules.** E1732 a `// Spec:` header line not of the Citation form, naming no section, or whose quote cites no Conformance bullet of its section; the message holds the quote's first six words and the nearest bullet `<section>#<n>`, the one sharing the most distinct words of the normal forms, the first on a tie · E1733 two Conformance bullets of one section with one normal form · W1733 a project's tests or page routes with no run report the ledger can use.

**Conformance.**
- A section `k.x` whose Conformance list is `- A is one.` gives the item `k.x#` and the first 8 hex digits of the SHA-256 of `a is one`, with `n` 1; with `- B is two.` added before it, the same item with `n` 2.
- The bullets ``- `A` is “one”.`` and `- a  is "one"` have one key.
- Over a committed ledger holding `k.x`'s clause `A is one.` at `example`, rewording it to `A is two.` makes `keel ledger --diff` report no E1706, and, with `k.x` `conformance-covered` and no file citing `A is two.`, E1707 at that bullet's line naming `k.x#1`.
- `// Spec: k.x, clause "a is one"`, and `// Spec: k.x, clause "A is` with `// one."` on the next line, each cite `A is one.`; `// Spec: k.x, clause "A is"` cites nothing and is E1732 at its line.
- The bullet `` `"hi"` is shown.`` is cited by `// Spec: k.x, clause "\"hi\" is shown."`; `// Spec: k.x, clause ""hi" is shown."` is E1732.
- With bullets `A is one.` and `B is two now.` in `k.x`, `// Spec: k.x, clause "B is two."` is one E1732 whose message holds `B is two.` and `k.x#2`.
- `// Spec: k.nope, clause "A is one."`, `// Spec: k.x Rules, "A is one."` and `// Spec: k.x, clause "A is one." "B is two now."` are each one E1732.
- Bullets `A is one.` and `a is one` in one section are one E1733 at the second's line, and the ledger holds one item for them.
- In package `p` whose `conformance/a.test.ts` cites `A is one.` with one test from line 3 to line 5: with `packages/p/reports/tests/run.json` holding `conformance/a.test.ts` at the file's `sha` with `{ line: 3, state: 'passed' }`, the clause is `example`; with `failed` or `skipped` in place of `passed`, or `line: 7`, it is `none`; with another `sha`, or no `reports/tests/`, it is `none` and `keel ledger` reports one W1733 at `packages/p/reports/tests/`.
- An `it.each([1, 2])` test whose report holds two tests at its line, one `passed` and one `failed`, gives no evidence; with both `passed` it gives `example`.
- A test file held at its `sha` by both `reports/tests/run.json` and `reports/tests/1-of-2.json` gives no evidence, and `keel ledger` reports one W1733.
- An export of `packages/p/src/f.ts` referenced only by a test its report records `failed` is `none`.
- In a folder holding `apps/a` whose `apps/a/reports/mutation/mutation.json` holds `modules/m/f.ts` with the tree's source and one Killed mutant, `keel ledger --write` stores a claim for `apps/a/modules/m/f.ts` and a `mutation.projects` entry `apps/a`; an `apps/a/mutation-justified.json` entry that justifies no mutant of that report is E1725.
- Run in a project folder whose `reports/mutation/mutation.json` holds `modules/m/f.ts` with the tree's source and one Killed mutant, `keel ledger --write` stores a claim for `modules/m/f.ts` and a `mutation.projects` entry `.`.
